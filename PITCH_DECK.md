### Slide 2: The Problem
1. **Centralized Data Lock-In:** Traditional invoicing tools (QuickBooks, FreshBooks) trap critical financial history behind monthly subscriptions and walled gardens.
2. **Subscription Drain:** Small freelancers and digital agencies pay \$200–\$600/year just to create basic PDFs.
3. **Disputes & Verification:** Clients and contractors frequently dispute delivery dates, due dates, and settlement claims with no neutral source of truth.
4. **Privacy Risks:** Legacy platforms expose sensitive personal details (home address, phone number) across insecure databases.

---

### Slide 3: The Solution — InvoiceMint
A lightweight, decentralized invoicing application built natively on Botchain:
- **100% On-Chain Registry:** Invoices, timestamps, and payment statuses are minted directly to smart contracts.
- **Zero Backend / Serverless:** No SQL databases, user sign-ups, or third-party tracking.
- **Client-Ready PDF Generation:** Built-in print engine transforms on-chain data into professional client invoices.
- **Privacy-Conscious Architecture:** Designed around customer aliases and business labels, avoiding PII leaks.

---

### Slide 4: How It Works (User Journey)
1. **Connect:** Connect MetaMask to Botchain Mainnet (Chain ID 677).
2. **Mint:** Enter customer name, project description, amount (BOT), and due date $\rightarrow$ mint invoice to chain.
3. **Manage:** Track total invoiced amounts, outstanding balances, and settlement rates in real-time.
4. **Export & Settle:** Download print-perfect PDF invoices or mark invoices as "Paid" once settled.

---

### Slide 5: Product Features
- **Real-Time Analytics Dashboard:** Total invoices, paid settlement rate %, unpaid balances, and gross volume.
- **Multi-Filter Navigation:** Filter by *All*, *Unpaid*, *Paid*, *Overdue*, and *Cancelled*, plus instant search.
- **Decentralized Access Control:** Only the issuing wallet can modify or cancel an invoice.
- **One-Click Print/PDF:** High-contrast invoice layout optimized for tax compliance and client delivery.
